Site Runbook

How to keep travelwrangell.com updated without calling anyone. Written for JR and Kate. Every task is marked with who can safely do it.

Go ahead

Safe to do anytime. Hard to break anything.

Careful

Doable — but follow the steps exactly and check the live site after.

Ask Matt

Don't attempt. Fast for him, risky to guess at.

Two habits that prevent most problems

1. Always check the live site, not the editor. Save, then open travelwrangell.com in a separate tab. Some things — forms especially — look broken while editing and are perfectly fine live.

2. Log what you changed. Add a line to the changelog at the bottom. When something breaks three weeks later, the log is how we find out what happened.

Section 01Everyday Content

Change words on an existing page

Go ahead

Click into the text and type. Squarespace saves it like a document.

One rule: don't change the size or style of a heading while you're in there. The big/small text settings control how Google reads the page. Change the words, leave the formatting.

Swap a photo

Go ahead
  1. Click the image, choose the replacement.
  2. Fill in the alt text box — one plain sentence describing the photo.

Alt text is what screen readers announce and what Google indexes. Skipping it is the single most common accessibility miss on the site.

Add a blog post

Go ahead

Blog is More About Wrangell, under Explore More.

  • Give it one category, picked from the ones that already exist. Categories are how people browse, and a new category with one post in it is a dead end. Don't create new ones.
  • Add a feature image, with alt text.

Aim for something every couple of months. A blog whose newest post is a year old reads as an abandoned site, which is worse than having no blog.

Update event dates

Go ahead

Every event page has a black band right under the photo with When / Where / Cost. That's the first thing visitors look for.

The rule: never publish an event page without a year in it. "Late July" is useless to someone planning a trip in February.

Update what happens at an event

Go ahead

The row of small boxes ("Downtown Parade," "Logging Show," "Fireworks") is built for exactly this. Edit the words, add or delete boxes. The layout takes care of itself.

Update the Business Directory

Go ahead

Add, remove or edit listings freely.

The most valuable thing you can add is hours and whether a business is open in winter. That's the number one question visitors have and the site currently doesn't answer it.

Paste clean links. Links copied from Airbnb, VRBO or Facebook carry a long tail of tracking code after a ?. Delete everything from the ? onward. The link still works and it won't break when that code expires.

Section 02The Homepage

The homepage is a library index, not a brochure

Each entry in The Island Index pairs a plain name with hard facts — how far, how you get there, what season, what you'll see. Not adjectives.

Write "30 miles south on the mainland · boat or floatplane · permits July–August." Not "an unforgettable wildlife experience." Facts are what visitors act on and what AI search can actually use. Adjectives are invisible to both.

Update an Island Index entry

Go ahead

Click the small text under any index name and edit it.

Keep the format: short facts separated by the dot character. Distance, how to get there, season, what's notable. Aim for 4 facts, not sentences.

If a permit window or a season changes, this is the first place to fix it — it's the most-read text on the site.

Swap a homepage photo

Go ahead
  1. Replace the image.
  2. Add alt text — one plain sentence describing what's in it.
  3. Add the photographer credit in the caption field.

Alt text and credits aren't optional extras here — they're part of the deliverable. The photography is doing the emotional work on this site, so the text attached to it is doing the findability work.

Change the featured video

Careful

Swap the video, then add the transcript. A video with no transcript is invisible to search and unusable for anyone who can't hear it.

No transcript, don't publish it. Getting one is a five-minute job with any auto-caption tool.

Change where a homepage button goes

Careful

The buttons at the bottom of the homepage — Plan Your Trip and Plan Your Event — are the main path from browsing to planning.

  1. Click the button, change the link.
  2. Save, open the live homepage, and click the button yourself.

This is worth the extra click. In September the Plan Your Trip button pointed at a page that no longer existed, so the homepage's main button led to an error page. Nothing warns you when that happens.

Reorder or resize Island Index tiles

Ask Matt

The tiles are deliberately different sizes — that's the design, not an accident. Resizing one reflows the whole grid.

Changing which entry is the big feature is a reasonable seasonal request. Just ask rather than dragging.

Update the Instagram feed

Go ahead

Nothing to do — it pulls from @travelwrangell automatically. Post to Instagram and the homepage updates itself.

The feed comes through a service called Behold, connected to the Instagram account. Nobody needs to touch it day to day.

One thing worth knowing. If that feed is ever deleted and rebuilt in Behold it gets a new ID, and the block on the website is still looking for the old one. When that happens the Instagram section quietly disappears from the homepage rather than showing an error — that is deliberate, so visitors never see an empty box. But it does mean nobody gets told.

So: if the Instagram section vanishes from the homepage, it is the feed ID. Two-minute fix. Send it over.

The conditions strip (daylight, tide, river, weather, ferry)

Go ahead

The five coloured panels under the homepage hero. Four of them look after themselves — daylight is calculated, and the tide, river and weather pull live from NOAA, the USGS and the National Weather Service every time the page loads. Nobody updates those.

The one you edit is the ferry. It is plain text inside the block — the line that says something like See schedule · Alaska Marine Highway · Matanuska. Change the words, leave everything else.

If a panel says “Check” instead of a number, that source is down at the moment. The panel turns into a link to the source so visitors are never stuck. It fixes itself when the source comes back. If one has said “Check” for more than a day, send it over.

The station numbers inside the block — 9451204 for the tide, 15024800 for the river — are Wrangell's. Don't change them.

Update “The Year, All at Once”

Careful

Not on the homepage yet — skip this until it is. When it goes up:

The twelve-month strip. Each month is a short line of text — “Hooligan run. Birdfest Apr 21–25.” — and that text is what you edit, directly inside the Code Block.

  1. Open the Code Block and find the month. Each one starts with its name, like Apr.
  2. Change only the words between the tags. Leave the tags themselves.
  3. Save, then check the live page, not the editor.

Two things you can also change, carefully: the colour of a month's band — the word after data-k=, which is one of water, bears, fest or quiet — and whether a month links somewhere, which is the href= part. Five months already link to their pages.

The red “Today” marker and the highlighted month are worked out from the date. They are not something you set, and they are never wrong.

Section 03Pages & Navigation

Build a new page

Careful

Never start from a blank page. Find the page closest to what you want, duplicate it, and replace the content.

  1. Duplicate an existing page.
  2. Rename it and set the URL.
  3. Replace text and images.
  4. Check it on a phone before publishing.

Duplicating carries the styling with it. Starting blank means rebuilding the look by hand, and it never quite matches.

Add a page to the menu

Careful

Drag it into the right folder in the Pages panel.

A folder is not a clickable page. Clicking "Respectful Travel" just opens the dropdown. Whatever sits first inside the folder is what people land on — so never put a new page above the existing first item unless you mean to change the landing page.

Add a form

Careful

Separate one-page guide: Adding a Form to the Website. Four steps, no code knowledge needed.

Three things worth memorizing:

  • Only one iDSS form per page. A second one quietly fails.
  • Edit a form in iDSS, re-paste it in Squarespace. Editing changes the form's access code; the old paste keeps showing the form but stops collecting responses.
  • Keep the wrapper. Every form sits inside one line of code that gives it the Travel Wrangell look — the guide shows it. Without it the form works but shows up in iDSS's own green and grey.

Reorder or rename menu sections

Ask Matt

Renaming a section changes its web address. Old links, printed materials and Google results all break unless a redirect is set up at the same time.

Delete, merge or retire a page

Ask Matt

Same problem as renaming. When a page goes away, its address has to be pointed somewhere useful at the same moment, or everyone who arrives from Google or an old link gets an error page.

This happened during the rebuild: the old Gold Rush and Hiking & Camping pages were removed and their addresses went to error pages until redirects were added. A redirect is a two-minute job. Just ask before the page comes down, not after.

Section 04Seasonal Rhythm

Late winter — before the season

  • Confirm and publish all four festival dates for the year
  • Update the cruise ship calendar
  • Check the Business Directory for closures and new businesses
  • Refresh homepage photos to something seasonal

Fall — after the season

  • Remove or archive anything that says "this year"
  • Post a recap or two while it's fresh
  • Note which festival pages got the most traffic, and put next year's effort there
  • Update winter hours in the directory

Once a year — the link check

Click every link under Partners & Resources and make sure it still goes where it should. Other organizations redesign their sites without telling anyone, and dead links there are especially costly because locals use that section most.

Section 05Cultural Content

Three standards, no exceptions

1. Name them correctly. First mention on any page is Shtax'héen Kwáan Tlingit — with the accents. Copy and paste names from here rather than retyping:

Shtax'héen Kwáan · Kiks.ádi (no space after the full stop) · Naanyaa.aayi

A space that sneaks in after the full stop — Kiks. ádi — is often an invisible "non-breaking" space, so find-and-replace won't catch it. If it's wrong, delete the whole word and paste it fresh.

2. Link the source. Any page with Tlingit content links to the Wrangell Cooperative Association and where relevant SEALASKA.

3. Present tense. Tlingit culture in Wrangell is living and active — not historical. Write it that way.

Do not paraphrase, summarize or invent cultural content. If new cultural copy is needed, it comes from WCA. This is the one area where a well-meaning edit can do real damage.

Section 06Writing in Wrangell's Voice

The test

Read it out loud. If it could describe Sitka or Petersburg with a find-and-replace, rewrite it.

More of this: "This is a working waterfront, so you'll see plenty of people doing exactly that—working." · "Moss covers just about anything that stands still long enough." · "There's also a Bearfest Race. And a Salmon Toss. It's exactly what it sounds like."

Less of this: breathtaking · nestled · gateway to · unforgettable · hidden gem · once-in-a-lifetime · pristine · majestic · at your own pace · something for everyone

And drop the last paragraph. Most pages end with a summary that repeats what was just said. Cutting it almost always improves the page.

Section 08Don't Touch

These break the whole site, not just one page

  • Custom CSS panel (Website Tools → Custom CSS) — every page's styling lives in one file here. One stray character and the whole site loses its formatting.
  • Code Injection — analytics and tracking. Editing it breaks reporting silently.
  • The Community Hotline form fields — the Concern Type and Location dropdowns feed the reports that go to CBW administration. Changing the options breaks the reporting.
  • The station numbers inside the conditions strip — 9451204 and 15024800 are Wrangell's tide station and river gauge. Change them and the panels show somewhere else's water.
  • Page URLs on live pages — changing one breaks every existing link to it.
  • Anything you don't recognize. Nothing here is urgent enough to guess at.
